01. What is Phishing?

Definition

A deceptive technique used by cybercriminals to trick individuals into revealing sensitive information such as login credentials, credit card numbers, or corporate data.

The Impact

  • Financial Loss
  • Data Breaches
  • Identity Theft

02. Common Phishing Types

Spear Phishing

Targeted Attack

Focuses on a specific individual or organization using personal details found online.

Whaling

The Big Fish

Targets high-level executives (CEOs, CFOs) to authorize large wire transfers.

Smishing

SMS Phishing

Deceptive text messages containing malicious links or fake package tracking info.

Vishing

Voice Phishing

Fraudulent phone calls where attackers pretend to be IT or Bank support personnel.

Angler Phishing

Social Media

Attackers intercept customer service requests on Twitter or Facebook.

Pharming

DNS Poisoning

Redirects you to a fake site even if you type the correct URL in your browser.

04. The Psychology of Deception

⌛

Urgency

"Act now or your account is gone!" Bypass rational thought.

⚖️

Authority

Impersonating the CEO or IRS to demand compliance.

😨

Fear

Threatening legal action or account permanent loss.

💰

Greed

"You've won! Click to claim." Exploiting hope for gain.

05. The URL Test

Attackers use "Typosquatting" to trick your eyes. Can you spot the difference?

Legitimate
microsoft.com
Malicious
mircosoft.com

Pro Tip: Always hover your mouse over links before clicking to see the actual destination in the bottom corner of your screen.

06. Your Defense Toolkit

📱

Multi-Factor (MFA)

Even if an attacker steals your password, they can't get in without the secondary token on your device.

🗝️

Password Managers

Generate and store unique, complex passwords for every site. If one site is breached, the others remain safe.

📣

Reporting Protocol

Don't just delete a phish. Use your company's "Report Phish" button. This alerts IT to block the sender for everyone else.
